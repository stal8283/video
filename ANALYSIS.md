## 1. The script formula

The script runs about 200 words in 78 s, roughly 155 wpm. It sounds conversational, with no "narrator voice."

| Beat | Time | Line | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0–5 s | "Imagine you go hiking with nine of your friends and every single one of them dies without explanation." | Second person ("you"). It puts the viewer *in* the story and gives the shocking outcome in the first sentence. |
| **Reality stamp** | 5–8 s | "Well, this actually happened in **1959**." | Turns the hypothetical into true crime. The year gets a big on-screen pop. |
| **Normal setup** | 8–17 s | 10 experienced hikers, Ural Mountains, one turns back | Establishes credibility ("highly experienced") so the later weirdness hits harder. |
| **Turn** | 17–26 s | "...which is when everything went downhill." / "what they discovered made absolutely no sense." | A curiosity gap before every reveal. |
| **Escalating clues** | 26–70 s | tent cut from inside → barefoot → bodies → broken branches 15 ft high → missing eyes/tongue → radiation | Each clue is stranger than the last. Connectors: "But it only got weirder", "But that wasn't all", "But the craziest of all". |
| **Anti-ending** | 70–78 s | "...concluded that they all died from a natural force of nature." | The official answer is unsatisfying, which pushes people into the comments (1.9K) and makes the loop feel unfinished. |

Key rules:
- **Every 3–5 seconds there is a "But..."**. That's about 8 escalation pivots in 78 s.
- **Specific, concrete numbers** ("nearly 15 ft high", "two months later", "1959"), never vague claims.
- **There's no intro, no "subscribe", and no channel name.** The video starts on the first word.

## 2. The visual formula

- **Signature presenter.** The creator, always in the same grey hoodie, is composited into every scene. They stand in the location and react: arms crossed, pointing, kneeling by a body. That's the brand. It's instantly recognizable in the feed.
- **Cartoon-face characters.** The historical people are realistic bodies with flat, round cartoon faces pasted on: blank white eyes, no expression. This makes grim content YouTube-safe and gives it a slightly funny tone. Victims lying in the snow are the same cartoons, so the result is "creepy but watchable."
- **AI / 3D-rendered environments.** Snowy forest, Soviet cabins, the tent interior, footprints, a drone shot of the ravine. Cinematic, cold colour palette.
- **About one cut per second.** I counted ~95 distinct frames in 78 s. The shot changes on almost every key noun.
- **Literal visuals.** "Tent cut open from the inside" shows the slashed tent from inside. "Footprints" is a top-down shot of footprints. "Fire" shows a fire. Viewers never have to imagine anything.
- **Captions.** One word at a time, small, in a dark pill near the top of the frame. Only one big text graphic in the whole video: **"1959"**, centre screen.
- **End frame.** The Soviet investigators, cartoon-faced with arms crossed, all in a row. A visual punchline that matches "natural force of nature."

## 3. Why it performs

1. The **first sentence is the entire premise**, so scroll-stop happens immediately.
2. **Constant novelty**: a new image every second plus a new clue every ~5 s.
3. The **unresolved ending** drives comments ("it was the Yeti", "Soviet weapons test") and rewatches.
4. **Consistent packaging** (the hoodie guy, cartoon faces, "The X Incident..." title with an ellipsis) builds a recognizable series.
